Accelerate Patent Prosecution: Strategic Approaches to Reducing Examination Timelines Across Major Jurisdictions

Nishith Desai Associates

Australia, Canada, China, European Union, Global, India, Japan, United Kingdom, USA October 15 2025

Patent prosecution timelines present a critical challenge for business operating in fast-moving technology sectors. The delay between filing and grant, often spanning several years, can undermine competitive positioning, delay investment rounds, and create uncertainty in licensing negotiations. For startups seeking to establish market dominance or established companies defending technological leadership, prolonged prosecution periods present both opportunity costs and strategic vulnerabilities.

While the fundamental legal and administrative requirements of patent examination cannot be eliminated, strategic management of the prosecution process can significantly reduce timelines. This article examines comparative examination frameworks across major patent offices, analyzes acceleration mechanism available to applicants, and provides practical guidance for developing multi-jurisdictional filing strategies that minimize pendency while optimizing resource allocation.

The five largest patent offices – collectively known as the IP5 (United States Patent and Trademark Office, European Patent Office, Japan Patent Office, Korean Intellectual Property Office, and China National Intellectual Property Administration), process approximately 90% of global patent applications. Examination timelines vary substantially across these jurisdictions, reflecting differences in examination procedures, resource allocation, and procedural frameworks.

Japan Patent Office: Fastest overall Processing

The JPO maintains the shortest average timeline among IP5 offices, with approximately 9.5 months from request for examination to first office action and 14 months to final disposition. Under accelerated examination procedures, first actions can be issued within 2.2 months. As of fiscal year 2023, the JPO employed 1,663 patent examiners and has implemented robust quality assurance mechanisms throughout the examination process.

Korean Intellectual Property Office: Efficient Digital Infrastructure

KIPO reports an average of 16.1 months to first office action, maintaining relatively quick processing times through technological innovation. The office has introduced an Online Examination Response Reservation System and enhanced chatbot consultation features to improve correspondence quality and user convenience. As of year-end 2023, KIPO employed 980 patent and utility model examiners.

European Patent Office: Fast Search, Extended Examination

The EPO prioritizes rapid search report generation, typically delivering results within five months. However, the overall timeline to grant averages 24.9 to 36 months, with some applications extending to 441. Months. This structure reflects the EPO's emphasis on comprehensive prior art searching as foundation for subsequent examination. The office employs around 4000 examiners working across search, examination, and opposition functions.

China National Intellectual Property Administration: Continuous Improvement

CNIPA has reduced average pendency for invention patents to approximately 16 months as of 2023, with faster processing for high-value applications. The office reports an examination accuracy rate of 94.2 percent, reflecting systematic efforts to balance speed with quality. Processing times vary based on technical field and strategic importance, with certain sectors receiving prioritized treatment.

United States Patent and Trademark Office: High Volume, Structured Goals

The USPTO traditionally maintained the longest pendency among IP5 offices, with approximately 17 to 20 months to first office action and 22 to 24 months to final disposition for utility applications. In 2021, the office transitioned to compliance-based framework targeting 14 months from filing to first action and 36 months to final action, aligning with the statutory mandates. The USPTO employs around 9000 utility, plant, and reissue patent examination staff.

Examination Request Procedures

The critical procedural distinction affects timing calculations across offices. The USPTO automatically examines every non-provisional patent application upon filing, resulting in 100 percent examination rate.. In contrast, the EPO, JPO, KIPO, and CNIPA require separate explicit requests for examination. At the EPO, the examination rate remains high because nearly all patent cooperation Treaty (PCT) applications entering the granting procedure proceed to examination. The JPO and KIPO demonstrate somewhat lower examination rates due to deferred examination systems that allow applicants extended time to assess commercial viability before incurring examination costs.

India: Emerging as Fast-Track Jurisdiction for first office action issuance

The Indian Patent Office (IPO) has undergone remarkable transformation in recent years. With approximately 833 patent examiners and 533 new recruits, the office has dramatically reduced examination timelines. According to world intellectual property organization (WIPO) indicators for 2024, the time taken to issue a First Examination Report (FER) has decreased from an average of six years (72 months) in 2015 to 10.6 months in 2023, positioning India among the fastest offices globally for the first office action. Similarly, time to final patent disposal has improved from an average of 84 months in 2015 to 49.5 months in 2023.

Under India's Expedited Examination System, applications are frequently decided within approximately one year of filing the request, with the fastest patent being granted at 41 days from the filing of examination request. (Note: Indian patent law mandates no grant of patent at least 6 months after publication). However, recent annual report indicates a concerning decline in issued FERs, dropping from 80,000 in FY 2019-20 to 18,000 in FY 2023-24. This decline suggests capacity constraints that may require infrastructure enhancement and process reform to maintain improved timelines.

Acceleration Mechanisms: A Comparative Framework

1. The Patent Prosecution Highway (PPH): Cross-Jurisdictional Efficiency

The Patent Prosecution Highway represents the most effective acceleration mechanism for multi-jurisdictional prosecution. PPH programs allow applicants who receive favorable examination results in one office to request expedited examination in participating offices, leveraging the substantive work already completed.

IP5 Patent Prosecution Highway

Within the IP5 framework, applications found patentable in one member office can receive priority examination in partner offices. The IP5 offices maintain a collective target of three months for issuing a first office action on PPH applications from the date the request becomes complete. Recent rule modifications provide additional flexibility: the office of the first filing (OFF) can be different from the office of earlier examination (OEE), provided that both OFF and OEE have PPH agreements signed with the regional Patent office. For example, an applicant may submit a PPH request in Brazil based on a patent granted by the JPO (OEE), even when priority was filed with the EPO (OFF).

Bilateral Patent Prosecution Highway Programs

Individual countries maintain bilateral PPH pilot programs that extend beyond the IP5 framework. The USPTO operates active bilateral programs with France, Malaysia, and Taiwan, and other jurisdictions. India has established a bilateral PPH with JPO, creating opportunities for applicants to leverage Japanese examination results for accelerated Indian prosecution.

Global Patent Prosecution Highway program

Launched in 2014, the Global PPH establishes a multilateral framework with harmonized set of rules and procedures across approximately 26 members countries. Participating offices include the USPTO, JPO, UKIPO, Canadian Intellectual Property Office, and IP Australia, among others. This standardization simplifies procedural requirements for applicants pursuing protection across multiple Global PPH jurisdictions.

PCT -Patent Prosecution Highway program

Applicant who receives positive International Seach Authority (ISA) reports and International Preliminary Report on Patentability during the PCT phase may request accelerated examination in countries with PCT-PPH agreements. 2. Expedited Examination Programs by Jurisdictions

Most major patent offices offer expedited examination programs, either universally or for specific categories. Fee structures and eligibility criteria vary substantially.

India: Category Based Expedited Examination

Indian patent law was amended to include eight categories of applicants: application with at least one Female applicant, Startup, Small and Medium Enterprises, PCT application designating India as ISA/IPEA, Government institutions, and several other categories The categorical approach reflects policy priorities to support specific applicant groups and technology sectors.

USPTO: Track One Prioritized Examination:

The USPTO's Track One program targets final disposition within 12 months of filing. USPTO anticipates increasing annual track one capacity to 20,000 applications in 2025. Effective July 2025, the USPTO is discontinuing the accelerated Examination program for utility applications, consolidating acceleration options under Track One. Recent pilot programs, including the First-Time Filer Expedited Examination Pilot Program (closed March 2025) and After Final Consideration Pilot Program 2.0 (closed December 2024), are no longer operational

European Patent Office (EPO): PACE Program

The EPO's Program for Accelerated Prosecution of European Patent Applications allows applicants to request both an expedited search and examination. Entry depends on EPO capacity in the relevant technical area. Under PACE, the EPO generates search reports "as soon as possible," and aims to produce examination results within three months of request.

Canada: Advancement as a Matter of Right

Canadian applicants can request advancement out-of-turn as a matter of right upon payment of a CAD 500 fee, providing straightforward access to acceleration without complex eligibility requirements

China: Strategic Sector Prioritization

CNIPA provides prioritized examination for strategic sectors including green technology, internet, big data, and applications facing infringement. Approved applications receive first office actions within 45 days and final decisions in about a year, representing substantial acceleration over standard timelines.

Japan: Commercial Implementation Criterion

Japanese prosecution can be expedited if an applicant or licensee of the application is actively using or selling the invention in Japan. This practical implementation criterion aligns acceleration with commercial deployment, ensuring expedited resources support economically active innovations.

United Kingdom: Grounds-Based Acceleration

UK applicants must provide reasonable grounds for expedited examination, such as potential infringement, commercial urgency, or manufacturing readiness. The UK intellectual property office aims to produce the first examination report within two months of request, making it an attractive European entry point for applicants requiring rapid certainty.

3. Regional Cooperation Frameworks

ASEAN Patent Examination Cooperation (ASPEC) Program

The ASPEC program allows applicants to leverage search and examination ("S&E") reports from participating IP Offices to accelerate the prosecution across nine Southeast Asian jurisdictions: Brunei Darussalam, Cambodia, Indonesia, Lao PDR, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Viet Nam. The program prioritizes Industry 4.0 patent applications, targeting first office actions within 6 months. This regional cooperation addresses capacity constraints in smaller offices while promoting technological development across the ASEAN economic community.

4. Technology-Specific Acceleration Programs

Green Technology and Climate Change Initiatives

Multiple jurisdictions have established accelerated examination programs for environmentally beneficial technologies. The USPTO has expanded its Climate Change Mitigation Pilot Program, advancing qualifying applications out of turn to expedite the first round of examination. The Indian Patent Office introduced expedited examination for green technology patents under Rule 24C of the Patents Rules, 2003 (amended 2024), reducing average examination time from 50 months to 12–18 months for qualifying applications.

Healthcare related Technologies

Brazil, Republic of Korea, Russian Federation, Thailand, United States of America provide accelerated examination for patent applications concerning health related technologies. These programs reflect policy priorities to expedite access to medical innovations, particularly following the COVID-19 pandemic.

First Filing and Commercial Implementation

Several Jurisdictions including Brazil, China, the Czech Republic, Japan, Norway, Singapore, and Thailand, offer expedited examination for applications first filed in the respective country. Additional jurisdictions, including Australia, Brazil, China, Israel, Japan, Norway, and the Republic of Korea, provide acceleration based on commercial implementation, production, export, or licensing activities..

5. Patent Grant Reciprocity

Certain countries facilitate patent grants based on examination results from partner offices.

  • Laos has implemented cooperation programs with Japan, China, and Korea to facilitate patent grant based on examination results from those jurisdictions, as well as re-registration from a Singaporean patent. These reciprocity arrangements provide cost-effective patent protection in markets with limited examination infrastructure.

Patent acceleration strategies must align with specific business objectives, resource constraints, and market priorities. No universal approach serves all applicants equally. Strategic optimization requires understanding the desired outcome and making informed decisions at each stage of the patent lifecycle.

2. Foundational Considerations

Jurisdiction Selection Based on Examination Speed

For applicants requiring rapid certainty in specific markets, selecting jurisdictions with inherently fast examination timelines provides foundational acceleration. The JPO's consistently short timelines make Japan an attractive jurisdiction for obtaining early allowances that can be leveraged through PPH programs. Similarly, the EPO's rapid search reports provide valuable prior art analysis that informs prosecution strategy across multiple jurisdictions, even though final grants may require extended timelines.

Cost-Benefit Analysis of Acceleration

Expedited examination programs typically require additional fees ranging from moderate (Canada's CAD 500) to substantial (USPTO Track One). Beyond direct fees, acceleration compresses response timelines, potentially requiring more intensive attorney engagement and reducing flexibility for strategic amendments. Applicants must weigh these costs against the value of early grant certainty, market protection, and potential revenue acceleration.

Resource Allocation and Timing

Large entities with substantial patent portfolios may benefit from immediate, high-cost filings across multiple jurisdictions. For startups and resource-constrained applicants, a phased approach often provides better risk management. Filing a PCT application defers expensive national phase entry by 30 months while preserving international filing dates. This delay allows time for market validation, technical refinement, and fundraising before committing to multi-jurisdictional prosecution costs.

Illustrative Prosecution Strategies

Illustrative Strategy One: Direct National Filing with PPH Leverage

Scenario: A biotechnology startup with an Indian priority application requires market presence in the United States and Japan within 18 months to support Series A fundraising and partnership discussions.

Strategic Approach:

  • Skip PCT filing to eliminate the 30-month deferral period
  • File directly in Japan, the United States, and India
  • Request expedited examination in Japan, targeting allowance within 14 months
  • Upon Japanese grant, file PPH requests in the United States and India
  • Target complete prosecution across all three jurisdictions within 30 months

Why this approach: This aggressive strategy prioritizes speed over cost optimization. Direct national filing eliminates PCT delays, and Japanese examination, the fastest among major offices, provides a foundation for PPH acceleration in other jurisdictions. The higher initial filing costs are justified by the compressed timeline meeting critical business milestones.

What to Expect: Most patents prosecuted within three years, with Japanese grant potentially achieved within 14 months. Initial filing costs exceed PCT-based alternatives, but timeline compression supports business objectives.

Scenario: A software company with moderate resources seeks global protection across the United States, Europe, Japan, China, and several secondary markets. The company requires reliable patentability assessment before committing to expensive national phase prosecution.

Strategic Approach: * File PCT application claiming Indian (or other suitable) priority * Designate JPO or USPTO as International Search Authority * Evaluating International Search Report and Written Opinion before national phase decisions * Enter national phase in jurisdictions showing strongest market potential * Use favorable ISA results to support PCT-PPH requests in participating offices

Why this approach: PCT filing defers national phase costs by 30 months while preserving international filing dates. Selecting JPO or USPTO as ISA provides high-quality prior art searching from offices with robust examination infrastructure. A positive ISR/Written Opinion may help with national phase strategy and enable PCT-PPH acceleration in multiple jurisdictions. This approach balances cost management with strategic flexibility.

What to Expect: Reliable patentability assessment before significant national phase investment. Successful applications leverage PCT-PPH for acceleration across multiple jurisdictions. Timeline extends 30 months beyond direct filing but with substantially reduced financial risk.

Illustrative Strategy Three: Sequential Acceleration Across Multiple Jurisdictions

Scenario: A manufacturing company seeks comprehensive global protection across major markets and secondary jurisdictions with moderate timelines.

Strategic Approach: * File PCT application with favorable ISR/Written Opinion * Enter national phase in Australia and Canada with expedited examination requests * Simultaneously request EPO PACE examination * Secure allowances in Australia, Canada, or Europe within 6–8 months of national phase entry * Leverage allowances to file PPH requests in Japan, the United States, Korea, and other target jurisdictions * Create cascading acceleration across remaining jurisdictions

Why this approach: This sequential strategy uses faster jurisdictions (Australia, Canada) as steppingstones to accelerate prosecution in major markets. Multiple early allowances provide flexibility as the PPH requests can be based on whichever jurisdiction grants first. The cascading approach distributes prosecution costs over extended periods while maintaining aggressive timelines.

What to Expect: Comprehensive global protection with substantially reduced overall pendency. Early grants in expedited jurisdictions provide business certainty while major market prosecution continues. Potential time savings of several years compared to conventional prosecution.

Risk Considerations and Limitations

Compressed Response Timelines

Expedited examination compresses the time available for substantive responses to office actions. While standard examination may allow several months for response preparation, accelerated tracks often require responses within shortened periods. This compression can limit opportunities for claim refinement, experimental data generation, or strategic consultation with inventors and business stakeholders.

Examination Quality Concerns

Accelerated examination places time pressure on examiners, potentially affecting the thoroughness of prior art searching or claim analysis. While major offices maintain quality standards across all examination tracks, applicants should recognize that expedited processes may receive less examiner time than standard examination, potentially increasing the risk of overlooked prior art or overly narrow claim constructions.

PPH Limitations and Restrictions

PPH programs require substantial correspondence between the claims in the PPH request and the allowed claims in the OEE. This requirement can limit prosecution flexibility, particularly when business objectives suggest pursuing broader or narrower claims in different jurisdictions. Additionally, PPH coverage remains incomplete across all office pairs, requiring careful verification of specific bilateral relationships.

Strategic Disclosure Considerations

Accelerated examination necessarily accelerates publication timelines, potentially disclosing technical information to competitors earlier than conventional prosecution. For technologies requiring extended development periods before commercialization, early publication may provide competitors with insights that inform their own development efforts.

Key Takeaways

  • The Patent Prosecution Highway represents the most effective acceleration mechanism, enabling multi-jurisdictional prosecution within two to three years when properly leveraged across IP5 offices
  • Japan offers the fastest timelines for initial office actions globally, making them strategic choices for obtaining early allowances that unlock PPH opportunities in major markets.
  • PCT filing with JPO or USPTO as International Search Authority balances cost management and risk mitigation, providing reliable patentability assessment before committing to expensive national phase prosecution
  • Effective acceleration strategies require matching mechanisms to specific business objectives - there is no one size that fits all!
